The Daydream

The sheets of chapter 15 are white-on-transparent principle drawings — they can teach the clocks, but not the lamp. This page is the colour half of the manhua/manhwa register: the shine band, the rim, the glisten, the palettes, and the staging of a slow turn. Everything here runs offline, in this one file.

The one rule underneath everything: three clocks on one head.

The paint belongs to the head — strand lines, features, drops of dew are texture, and ride the turn exactly as far as the skull turns. The tips belong to physics — chains, late, overshooting where the roots stop. The shine belongs to the lamp — recomputed from the light's direction every frame, never animated. The head slides under the light; the light does not follow. Hand-animating the shine is the classic error: it glues the lamp to the skull, and hair reads as laminated.

1Three clocks on one head

Sheet 15·03, in colour and alive. Drag the turn, or press play. Then switch the layers off one at a time and watch what each clock owns: the strand lines ride the skull, the tips hang from their chains and answer late, and the bright band stands exactly where the lamp put it — +12.6 px right of centre, on every frame.

The band sits at a bearing of 35° off the head's centre — never dead centre. A centred band reads as a stripe painted onto the hair.

2Anatomy of the shine band

Two dials decide what a band of shine reads as: its bearing (where the lamp puts it) and its width (how quickly it falls off). The third control is the classic error, included so you can watch it fail: glue the shine to the head and turn — the lamp slides with the skull, and the hair laminates.

3The rim — a figure against the lamp

Sheet 15·04's rule, drawn big: the rim is recomputed from the geometry every frame. For each segment of the silhouette, measure how dead-on its edge faces the lamp (a facing from 0 to 1); where the facing passes 0.25, paint an offset curve on the lamp side with alpha = 0.85 · ((facing − 0.25) / 0.75)1.5. The power is the secret — dead-on is much brighter than nearly dead-on, so the rim flickers as the figure breathes through the light.

Move the lamp and watch the rim leave the dark side without being told to. That is the whole recipe: the rim is a measurement, never a drawing.

4Glisten — dew crossing a sheen

Sheet 15·05. The sheen is soft, wide, and fixed in space (the lamp's). The drops are small, hard, and fixed to the skin (the face's). A drop that comes within 10° of the sheen shows; within 5° it winks — brightness 1.0, 0.6, 0.35, 0 over four frames at 24 fps, a four-point star on the brightest frame only. Because the drops sit at different bearings (+38°, +16°, +4°), they cross one after another: a cascade, not a shimmer.

Watch the far drop (+38°) wink first, at a turn of −10°; then +16°'s drop at +12°; then +4°'s at +24°. Three sparks strung along the turn — their order is set by geometry, not by a timeline. A wink lasts a sixth of a second; longer reads as a sticker.

5Palettes — four lamps

The register lives in restraint: the base hair is always darker than you think, the band is near-white but narrow, the rim takes the lamp's colour rather than the hair's, and the skin's sheen is a whisper of the lamp over the base cheek. Four lamps to start from — steal them, then break one rule at a time.

Usage: band is drawn at alpha ≈ 0.65 over the hair with a 4.2 px falloff; rim is additive, alpha by facing (section 3); sheen at alpha ≈ 0.28, a soft ellipse on the cheek; dew winks to full white for one frame. Keep the page background near the world colour — a daydream figure is always lit against something.

6Staging the turn — three springs, three decisions

Sheet 15·09's timing laid flat. A glance is three drawn decisions, each given to its own spring (a value with velocity that chases a target): the yaw (the turn itself) is asked for −8° at frames 2–4 — a small lie in the other direction, the anticipation — then 75°. The roll (the lean) is asked for +10° a frame before the yaw and keeps −5° afterwards: the lean leads the look, and the look keeps the lean. The pitch (the chin) dips 5° into the whip and rises 2° out of it. Drag the damping down and watch the overshoot grow — ζ 0.55 is what lets the yaw run 9° past its ask; that overshoot is the whip.

Reading the chart: dashed steps are the asks (the decisions), solid curves are the springs' answers. Frame 3: the lean starts; frame 4: the turn starts; ≈ frame 12: the yaw peaks past 75°; afterwards: the roll settles at −5° and stays. A glance that fully unleans is a robot resetting.
